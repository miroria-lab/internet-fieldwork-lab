A Village That Exists More in Satellite Images Than on Google Maps

I spent 25 minutes exploring Chong-Oruktu, a small village beside Lake Issyk-Kul in Kyrgyzstan.

I originally stopped here simply because I liked the sound of the place name. But the satellite view immediately looked strange.

1. Fields like the teeth of a comb

Between the mountains and the lake were dozens of extremely long, narrow strips of land.

Some were green. Others were brown. From above, the whole landscape looked almost like a giant comb stretching toward Lake Issyk-Kul.

Explore Chong-Oruktu

2. Hot spring pools beside the fields

Near the lakeshore I found Keremet-Suu, a hot spring facility with outdoor pools.

Mountains, farmland, hot water and an enormous lake all exist within a remarkably small area.

See the hot spring

3. Hundreds of homes — but almost nothing to search for

Zooming into the village revealed streets, houses, gardens and many signs of everyday life.

But searches for ordinary shops and restaurants produced surprisingly few results.

The village clearly exists physically, yet much of its everyday life seems almost invisible on the digital map.

4. A guest house built around trout

In the hills above the village I found a guest house with large ponds full of trout.

The photos showed fish ponds, accommodation, meals and a landscape shaped by water coming down from the mountains.

See the guest house

5. And then there was this fried fish

One photo showed a whole fish cut into repeated sections and fried until the pieces curled outward.

I had never seen fish prepared quite like this before.

Find the fish photos

The experiment

This is not a complete explanation of Chong-Oruktu.

It is simply what became visible during one 25-minute walk through a place I had never heard of before.

A strange place name led to farmland, hot springs, digital map gaps, trout farming and an unfamiliar way of frying fish.
